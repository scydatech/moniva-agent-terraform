# Moniva Procedure: Suspicious Activity and Account Takeover Escalation

Document ID: MNV-FRD-201 | Version: 3.1 | Owner: Head of Fraud and Compliance | Effective: 1 August 2026
Classification: Internal - Restricted | SAMPLE DOCUMENT FOR SYSTEM TESTING - FICTIONAL CONTENT

## 1. Red flags for possible account takeover
Treat the following combination as a possible account takeover and escalate immediately:
- a login from a new device or a password/PIN reset, followed within 24 hours by
- transfers to beneficiaries the customer has never paid before, and
- several transfers in a short period (three or more within one hour), or transfers totalling more than 50% of the daily limit.

Other red flags: changes to phone number or email shortly before transfers, transfers at unusual hours for the customer, and round-amount transfers just below limits.

## 2. What investigators do
1. Gather the evidence: the account's recent security events, the transfers after the event, the beneficiaries and whether they are new, and the totals.
2. Do not contact the customer about the suspicion and do not disclose that a fraud review is under way (no tipping off).
3. Escalate to the Fraud team through the fraud case queue within 30 minutes, with the evidence summary.

## 3. Account restrictions
Only the Fraud team may place or lift a restriction on an account:
- Post-No-Debit (PND) or account freeze: authorized by a Fraud Analyst, confirmed by the Fraud Team Lead within 2 hours.
- Lifting a restriction: authorized by the Fraud Team Lead after customer verification.
Operations staff must not place, lift or promise to lift a restriction.

## 4. Beneficiary bank recall
Where funds have left Moniva, the Fraud team requests a recall or lien from the beneficiary banks. Speed matters: requests should be sent within 1 hour of confirming suspected fraud.

## 5. Regulatory reporting
Compliance decides whether a suspicious transaction report is required. Investigators provide the evidence summary to Compliance through the fraud case; they do not file reports themselves.
